# Seven Laws of Reality

This document synthesizes the archive's `THE HERMETIC BASE — 7 LAWS OF REALITY`
page into canonical engine language.

## Function in the Pyramid

The Hermetic Base is the layer a participant returns to when they need to
understand the underlying structure beneath patterns, conflict, change, and
integration.

It is presented as a non-dogmatic framework:

- tools, not commandments
- mirrors, not cages
- a living structure, not a closed doctrine

## Canonical Base

The Hermetic Base remains the canonical framing for this layer.

Its primary structure is the seven Hermetic Principles in canonical Kybalion
order:

1. Mentalism
2. Correspondence
3. Vibration
4. Polarity
5. Rhythm
6. Cause and Effect
7. Gender

These principles remain the engine-level language of the Hermetic Base.

## Cross-System Translation Layer

For broader system readability, NE3ULA also uses a simplified philosophical
translation layer.

These statements are **not** replacements for the Hermetic Principles, and they
are **not** intended as a strict one-to-one renaming of the canonical base.
They function instead as an interpretive bridge: a way of rendering the
Hermetic layer legible across the wider NE3ULA system without collapsing its
depth or altering its canonical order.

The simplified translation layer is:

1. Truth is real, but access is partial.
2. Perception shapes experience.
3. Repetition becomes identity.
4. Action reveals alignment.
5. Polarity must be integrated.
6. The individual remains sovereign.
7. Everything must serve conscious becoming.

These statements should be read as cross-system orientation, not as a rewritten
version of the Hermetic Base itself.

## Principle Interpretations

### Mentalism

Reality is shaped through consciousness and perception.

Canonical engine use:
- thought affects direction
- interpretation shapes pathing
- awareness changes experience
- access to truth is always partial and mediated through perception

Cross-system resonance:
- truth is real, but access is partial
- perception shapes experience

### Correspondence

Inner and outer patterns reflect one another.

Canonical engine use:
- relational and environmental patterns mirror internal states
- structure outside often reflects structure within

Cross-system resonance:
- perception shapes experience
- action reveals alignment

### Vibration

Everything moves, resonates, or carries frequency.

Canonical engine use:
- emotional and energetic states matter
- rhythm and signal quality influence action
- repetition becomes embodied over time

Cross-system resonance:
- repetition becomes identity
- everything must serve conscious becoming

### Polarity

Opposites are linked rather than fully separate.

Canonical engine use:
- tension can be held rather than prematurely resolved
- contradiction is often developmental material
- mastery is integration, not domination

Cross-system resonance:
- polarity must be integrated

### Rhythm

Transformation moves in cycles rather than straight lines.

Canonical engine use:
- return is expected
- rest and motion belong together
- repeated phases are part of mastery

Cross-system resonance:
- repetition becomes identity
- everything must serve conscious becoming

### Cause and Effect

Actions and structures produce consequences.

Canonical engine use:
- habits matter
- choices compound
- systems generate outcomes
- action is the test of whether alignment is becoming real

Cross-system resonance:
- action reveals alignment

### Gender

The archive frames gender here as creative energetic polarity rather than as
social categorization.

Canonical engine use:
- initiating and receiving are both necessary
- balanced creation requires more than one kind of force

Cross-system resonance:
- polarity must be integrated
- everything must serve conscious becoming

## Design Constraint

This layer should always remain an interpretive foundation.
It should not be used as a coercive belief test inside E3.

The individual remains sovereign, and this layer must remain in service of
conscious becoming rather than submission to doctrine.
